# Not physical

English · [Русский](ru/not-physical.md)

Incoming uses physical models for blast effects, with approximations and estimates. These game rules deliberately
depart from real weapons; selecting REAL does not remove all of them.

| Part | Game rule |
|---|---|
| Launch and flight | Crafted munitions, nearby launchers and configurable approach times serve the game. Flight, guidance, turns, climb rates and target tracking use a game model. GAME uses game speeds and heights; REAL changes speeds and heights, while maneuvers stay game values. The B-2's default release height is 170 blocks rather than about 12 km. See [flight](weapons.md#how-they-fly). |
| Salvos | Shaheds, missiles and Lancets take separate lanes beside one route so the salvo can be read as a wave. Their aim points keep the same accuracy. See [flight](weapons.md#how-they-fly). |
| Grad | At game speed it can fire much closer than the real minimum range. Its visible motor trail is extended to the first 650 blocks of flight; at every scale it leaves the tube at its full calculated exit speed, without in-tube acceleration. Its 0.4° direction and 0.5% speed spread are game values; the trail ends at 650 blocks. See [trails](weapons.md#flames-and-smoke-trails). |
| Air defense | Radar/engagement ranges, target signatures, guidance, the 40 g turn limit, one motor with boost and sustain modes, 15 s flight limit and 6-block fuze are game values. REAL raises interceptor speed toward 1000 m/s; it does not turn the SAM into a real radar or guidance simulation. See [SAM](defense.md#surface-to-air-missile-system-sam). |
| ICBM | The final visible approach uses the shared game flight-speed scale; configured launch-to-burst time is unchanged. The warhead glows as bright as at its real entry speed, and its trail has the real length, while it moves at game speed; the approach is steeper than a real one. The first stage burns for its real 61 s and turns like the real one, but climbs at game speed, so it burns out about 22 km up instead of about 40; detonation uses a timer, 90 s by default rather than a real intercontinental flight of about 30 minutes. The strike has no random miss. See [launching a nuke](nuke.md#how-to-launch). |
| Nuclear scale | GAME and REAL keep full nuclear radii. CUSTOM can set `nuclear.effects_scale` from 0.005 to 1: below 1, damage and effect distances shrink. GAME slows the distant wave to 0.35 of the speed of sound; CUSTOM can set 0.15–1. These changes affect wave arrival and ruin timing, not just the picture. See [world settings](settings.md#world-settings). |
| Nuclear dust wave | Everywhere (every view, over water, with several detonations, at every setting except OFF) the blast wave's dust, from the front to the thin haze hours later, is drawn by one presentation model: its crest, settling, billows, their motion and light are shaped after real dust-storm footage. Each spot's dust overshoots to about one and a half times the layer's height before it settles back within a second, so a crest runs behind the wave. Its layer is drawn about 1.2 times taller than the physical one at first, so that at 4.5 km from a 1 Mt burst its crest stands a little below a camera 120 blocks up, and joins the physical layer's rising height over about three minutes. Thin haze is as dense as its physical optical depth; thick dust is drawn thinner, a matte, semi-transparent wall rather than an opaque skin. Inside old dust you see about 200 m after the first minute and about 700 m after five, then farther as it thins; physically the view would stay around 50 m for ten minutes. For the first minute the dust's crests lift as tufts up to a few hundred metres, more over the fire zone. Where and when the dust rises, how much, how it settles and thins, and the wind stay physical. See [what happens](nuke.md#what-happens). |
| Long-term effects | After the first 20 minutes, fires, black rain, fallout decay, injuries, hearing and power repairs follow the world's day: in a vanilla world an hour passes in 50 seconds; with the day cycle stopped, in real time. See [time after the strike](nuke.md#time-after-the-strike). |
| Fragment marks | A hit can turn an entire block into its cracked variant instead of leaving a small pit. Fragments pass through Create Aeronautics craft. See [blast limits](weapons.md#what-a-blast-does). |
| Sound coverage | Projectile sound paths cover at most 60 seconds of sound travel (about 20.6 km). Conventional blast packets stop at 32 km even when the sound model would carry farther. These are transmission/history limits. See [sound](defense.md#sirens). |
| Cleanup | Spent boosters disappear after 5 s on the ground or 20 s after separation, whichever comes first, with a 2 s fade. Their physical cooling can last longer than the visible body. See [trails](weapons.md#flames-and-smoke-trails). |

Presentation changes how effects read on screen: exposure, glow, contrast, billows and camera motion. It does not
change a charge or a damage radius. Model approximations are different again: for example, simplified blast
shielding and estimated fuel/casing properties are limits of the calculation, not hidden game multipliers.
