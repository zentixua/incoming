# Not physical

English · [Русский](ru/not-physical.md)

Incoming uses physical models for blast effects, with approximations and estimates. These game rules deliberately
depart from real weapons; selecting REAL does not remove all of them.

| Part | Game rule |
|---|---|
| Launch and flight | Crafted munitions, nearby launchers and configurable approach times serve the game. Flight, guidance, turns, climb rates and target tracking use a game model. GAME uses game speeds and heights; REAL changes speeds and heights, while maneuvers stay game values. The B-2's default release height is 170 blocks rather than about 12 km. A round leaving a launcher on a moving aircraft keeps only the aircraft's speed along its rails, not across them. See [flight](weapons.md#how-they-fly). |
| Salvos | Shaheds, missiles and Lancets take separate lanes beside one route so the salvo can be read as a wave. Their aim points keep the same accuracy. See [flight](weapons.md#how-they-fly). |
| Grad | At game speed it can fire much closer than the real minimum range. Its visible motor trail is extended to the first 650 blocks of flight; at every scale it leaves the tube at its full calculated exit speed, without in-tube acceleration. Its 0.4° direction and 0.5% speed spread are game values; the trail ends at 650 blocks. See [trails](weapons.md#flames-and-smoke-trails). |
| Air defense | Radar/engagement ranges, target signatures, guidance, the 40 g turn limit, one motor with boost and sustain modes, 15 s flight limit and 6-block fuze are game values. REAL raises interceptor speed toward 1000 m/s; it does not turn the SAM into a real radar or guidance simulation. See [SAM](defense.md#surface-to-air-missile-system-sam). |
| ICBM | The final visible approach uses the shared game flight-speed scale; configured launch-to-burst time is unchanged. The warhead glows as bright as at its real entry speed, and its trail has the real length, while it moves at game speed; the approach is steeper than a real one. The first stage burns for its real 61 s and turns like the real one, but climbs at game speed, so it burns out about 22 km up instead of about 40; detonation uses a timer, 90 s by default rather than a real intercontinental flight of about 30 minutes. The strike has no random miss. See [launching a nuke](nuke.md#how-to-launch). |
| Nuclear scale | GAME and REAL keep full nuclear radii. CUSTOM can set `nuclear.effects_scale` from 0.005 to 1: below 1, damage and effect distances shrink. GAME slows the distant wave to 0.35 of the speed of sound; CUSTOM can set 0.15–1. These changes affect wave arrival and ruin timing, not just the picture. See [world settings](settings.md#world-settings). |
| Nuclear dust wave | Everywhere (every view, over water, with several detonations, at every setting except OFF) the blast wave's dust, from the front to the thin haze hours later, is drawn by one presentation model: its crest, settling, billows, their motion and light are shaped after real dust-storm footage. Each spot's dust overshoots to about one and a half times the layer's height before it settles back within a second, so a crest runs behind the wave. Its layer is drawn about 1.2 times taller than the physical one at first, so that at 4.5 km from a 1 Mt burst its crest stands a little below a camera 120 blocks up, and joins the physical layer's rising height over about three minutes. Thin haze is as dense as its physical optical depth; the thick young wall is drawn thinner, a matte, semi-transparent wall rather than an opaque skin, and once it has settled into haze (half a minute to a minute after the wave) it is as dense as its physical optical depth again, up to the depth beyond which nothing shows through. Only for a camera inside the dust: in old dust you see about 200 m after the first minute and about 700 m after five, then farther as it thins (physically the view would stay around 50 m for ten minutes). Seen from outside, the dust keeps the density above, so from a hill or from above the settled blanket hides the ground for as long as it is thick. For the first minute the dust's crests lift as tufts up to a few hundred metres, more over the fire zone. Past your render distance Minecraft draws no ground, so a wave standing there on the ground would hang over a strip of sky: when you stand on the ground (up to about 4 m above the ground at the edge of the drawn world), the dust's foot is drawn down to that edge, where the horizon is on screen. From higher up, a hill or a roof, without a distant-terrain mod, the sky under it remains. Its tint varies slightly within the dust (darker coarse soil low, paler fines on the billow tops, patches a little redder or greyer): the shading follows what the dust is made of, its strength is chosen for the picture. Where and when the dust rises, how much, how it settles and thins, and the wind stay physical. See [what happens](nuke.md#what-happens). |
| Fire smoke | The town's smoke and its black rain move with the wind as in reality. A drifting stretch of smoke thinner than the visibility limit (95 % of the sunlight passing through) still shows faintly until it is half as thick, fading out instead of vanishing at once; black rain falls only from smoke at the limit or thicker. See [time after the strike](nuke.md#time-after-the-strike). |
| Long-term effects | After the first 20 minutes, fires, fallout decay, injuries, hearing and power repairs follow the world's day: in a vanilla world an hour passes in 50 seconds; with the day cycle stopped, at the pace the day last ran. A player's injuries and hearing pause while they are logged out. See [time after the strike](nuke.md#time-after-the-strike). |
| Wind | The world's wind takes a new direction and strength every 24 000 game ticks (20 minutes of play, one vanilla day), whatever the length of the day, and turns smoothly between them. With a mod that makes days 24 hours long, a world day has 72 winds instead of one. Sleep and `/time` do not move it, so the wind never swings at once. See [trails](weapons.md#flames-and-smoke-trails). |
| Flash shadows | Only walls, houses and hills within about 40 blocks toward the burst shade the ground from the flash; farther ridges and forests do not, so the scorch behind distant hills is stronger than in reality. See [what happens](nuke.md#what-happens). |
| Fragment marks | A hit can turn an entire block into its cracked variant instead of leaving a small pit. Fragments pass through Create Aeronautics craft. See [blast limits](weapons.md#what-a-blast-does). |
| Sound coverage | Projectile sound paths cover at most 60 seconds of sound travel (about 20.6 km). Conventional blast packets stop at 32 km even when the sound model would carry farther. These are transmission/history limits. See [sound](defense.md#sirens). |
| Cleanup | Spent boosters disappear after 5 s on the ground or 20 s after separation, whichever comes first, with a 2 s fade. Their physical cooling can last longer than the visible body. See [trails](weapons.md#flames-and-smoke-trails). |

Presentation changes how effects read on screen: exposure, glow, contrast, billows and camera motion. It does not
change a charge or a damage radius. Model approximations are different again: for example, simplified blast
shielding and estimated fuel/casing properties are limits of the calculation, not hidden game multipliers.
